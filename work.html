Honors Thesis jsPsych R 2025 to 2026

Techno on the Brain: Rhythmic Scaffolding and Visual Temporal Order Memory

My honors thesis asked whether techno synchronized with a sequence of images improves memory for the order of those images. In a within-subjects experiment (N = 59), participants completed 40 two-alternative temporal-order judgment trials across four auditory conditions: Sync, Out-of-sync, Metronome, and Ambient. Auditory condition had no effect on accuracy, and neither attentional control nor musical sophistication moderated it.

Role First Author
Methods jsPsych, Repeated-Measures ANOVA, Linear Mixed-Effects Models in R
Result Null: no effect of auditory condition on accuracy (p = .224)
Lab Memory & Decision Making Lab, UCSB (Advisor: Dr. Michael Miller)

Environmental Sound Effects & False Memory

A 2×2 between-subjects experiment (N = 67) for PSY 120L on whether environmental sound effects change false memory in the DRM paradigm. Sound effects had no effect. Confidence had a main effect (p = .048), and confidence correlated with false memory (r = .28, p = .021). The poster won Best Poster at the course colloquium.

Role Co-investigator, Team of Four
Methods Factorial ANOVA, Pearson Correlation, Qualtrics, SPSS
Result No effect of sound effects; main effect of confidence
Course PSY 120L, UC Santa Barbara
